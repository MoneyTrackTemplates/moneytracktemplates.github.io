₿ Crypto: Start at the Beginning

What exactly is cryptocurrency? What is a blockchain? Why does Bitcoin exist? What does a crypto wallet actually do? What is an exchange? And why does everyone keep talking about private keys?

The MoneyTrack crypto section will answer those questions in plain English before moving into more advanced subjects.

Topics we'll cover:

  • Cryptocurrency basics
  • Blockchain explained simply
  • Bitcoin and other digital assets
  • Crypto wallets
  • Private keys and seed phrases
  • Cryptocurrency exchanges
  • Buying, selling and transferring
  • Transaction and network fees
  • Stablecoins
  • Mining and staking
  • Taxes and record keeping
  • Volatility and financial risk

🛡️ Crypto Safety School

Before discussing potential opportunities, newcomers should understand the risks.

  • Learn the difference between traditional bank deposits and crypto assets.
  • Understand what private keys and recovery phrases do.
  • Learn why sending crypto can be very different from making an ordinary card payment.
  • Recognize promises of guaranteed profits as a warning sign.
  • Learn how impersonation, investment and romance scams can involve cryptocurrency.
  • Understand volatility before risking money.
  • Verify information before acting on social-media promotions or unsolicited messages.

Our rule:
Understand it before you put money into it.

Planned Feature

🔎 MoneyTrack Financial Research Desk

Eventually, visitors will be able to ask financial questions in ordinary language and use the Research Desk to investigate the subject.

The goal is not to tell someone what financial decision to make. The goal is to help the visitor understand the subject, examine relevant information, and see where that information came from.

“What is the difference between APR and APY?”
“How does a certificate of deposit work?”
“What happens when the Federal Reserve changes rates?”
“Explain a crypto wallet to someone who has never used one.”
“What is compound interest?”
“Where can I find official information about my credit report?”

Research first. Understand the evidence. Then make your own decision.

How We'll Build It

Sources Matter

Financial information is most useful when visitors can tell where it came from and how current it is.

Original Sources Whenever practical, we'll trace information back to the agency, regulator or original publisher.
Publication Dates Changing financial information should include a date or observation period when available.
Plain English Technical terminology will be explained rather than assumed.
Evidence vs. Interpretation Facts, estimates, opinions and interpretations should not be presented as though they are the same thing.

Educational Purpose

MoneyTrackTemplates provides educational and organizational information. Financial products, laws, tax rules, rates, regulations and market conditions can change.

Information on this site should not be treated as individualized financial, investment, tax or legal advice. When a decision depends on current rules or personal circumstances, verify the information with the appropriate official source or qualified professional.
