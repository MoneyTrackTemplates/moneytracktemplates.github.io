Activity 03 · Choose a level

Keep exploring at your own level

Early learners

Find and color three different leaf shapes. Count the acorns. Say which colors you would use for autumn.

Elementary

Finish the word search. Then write three sentences about what changes outside during autumn.

Middle school

Observe a local tree or plant. Record three details, sketch one change, and explain what evidence tells you the season is changing.

High school

Compare daylight or average temperature in two months using a reliable local source. Graph the results and explain the pattern.

College & adults

Research how one seasonal tradition has changed over time. Compare two sources, note their context, and cite them.

Flexible lesson guide

Teach the season in 30–45 minutes

Use this plan with one learner or a group. Adjust the activity to the learners’ age and interests.

Learning goals

  • Observe and describe seasonal changes.
  • Build vocabulary or research skills at an appropriate level.
  • Create an original response through art, writing, or data.

Materials

  • This printable page and pencils or crayons.
  • Optional: a leaf, a photo, a local weather record, or a library book.
  • For older learners: a trustworthy source for research.

Lesson flow

  1. Notice (5 min): Ask what learners observe changing outside.
  2. Explore (15–20 min): Color, solve, or research by level.
  3. Share (10 min): Explain a choice, word, pattern, or source.
  4. Reflect (5 min): Name one new thing learned.

Adapt & assess

For younger learners, talk and point instead of writing. For older learners, ask for evidence and a cited source. Check whether each learner can describe a seasonal change and support the observation.
