How to Keep Track of Monthly Subscriptions and Recurring Expenses

A beginner-friendly guide to understanding recurring charges, renewal dates, billing frequency, annual costs, free trials, and subscription tracking in Excel.

Subscriptions can be convenient.

You sign up once, enter a payment method, and the service continues automatically.

That convenience can also make subscriptions surprisingly easy to forget.

A few dollars for an app. A streaming service. Cloud storage. Music. Gaming. Software. A membership. An annual service you signed up for months ago.

Individually, the charges may not seem like much. Together, they can become a significant part of your monthly or yearly spending.

Your first goal:

Know what you're paying for, how much it costs, when you're charged, and whether you still use it.

You do not need complicated financial knowledge to do that, and you do not need to be an Excel expert.

What Is a Subscription?

A subscription is an arrangement in which you pay repeatedly for continued access to a product, service, membership, or benefit.

You might pay:

  • Weekly
  • Monthly
  • Every few months
  • Every six months
  • Annually

Examples may include:

  • Video streaming
  • Music streaming
  • Gaming memberships
  • Cloud storage
  • Software
  • Mobile apps
  • News or magazine subscriptions
  • Fitness memberships
  • Educational services
  • Professional memberships
  • Website services
  • Delivery memberships

If You're a Student, You May Already Have Subscriptions

Subscriptions are not only an adult household expense.

Middle- and High-School Students

  • Gaming subscriptions
  • Music streaming
  • Mobile apps

  • Study apps
  • Video streaming

College and Graduate Students

  • Productivity software
  • Online study tools
  • Professional software
  • Cloud storage

  • Gym memberships
  • Delivery memberships
  • News subscriptions

Learning how recurring charges work is useful even before you manage a large household budget.

A Few Subscription Words You Should Know

Recurring Expense

A recurring expense is a cost that happens repeatedly.

Billing Frequency

The billing frequency tells you how often you're charged.

Renewal Date

The renewal date is when the subscription renews for another billing period.

Auto-Renewal

Auto-renewal means a subscription is designed to renew automatically unless you take action according to the provider's terms.

Monthly Cost

The monthly cost is what the subscription costs during one month.

Annual Cost

The annual cost is what the subscription costs over approximately one year.

Free Trial

A free trial allows temporary access without paying, or sometimes at a reduced introductory price.

Step 1: Find Your Current Subscriptions

You might review:

  • Bank transactions
  • Credit-card statements
  • App-store subscriptions
  • Email receipts
  • PayPal or other payment activity
  • Membership accounts
  • Software accounts
  • Previous bills

Don't rely entirely on memory.

Step 3: Understand Monthly vs. Annual Billing

$10 per month × 12 months = $120 per year

For an annual subscription:

$96 per year ÷ 12 months = $8 average monthly cost

The $8 figure is useful for comparison. It does not mean the company will actually charge $8 each month.

Step 9: Review Whether You're Actually Using It

Ask yourself:

  • When did I last use this?
  • Do I use it regularly?
  • Is another subscription providing the same thing?
  • Has the price increased?
  • Do I still need it?
  • Is there a less expensive plan that meets my needs?
  • Is the subscription still worth the cost to me?
The goal is not to automatically cancel everything. The goal is to understand what you're paying for.

What About Family Plans?

Suppose a household pays $20 per month for one family plan used by four people.

The household cost is still $20 per month.

But if four people each have separate $20 subscriptions, the combined household cost would be $80.

What About Student Discounts?

Record the amount you're actually paying.

Also check whether:

  • The discount must be renewed
  • The discount expires
  • The price changes after graduation
  • An introductory rate lasts only for a certain period

What About Free Trials?

Free trials deserve their own place in your tracker.

Example:

Trial Start: September 1
Trial End: September 30
First Possible Paid Date: October 1
Expected Price: $12.99

Always check the provider's actual trial and billing terms.

What If the Subscription Price Changes?

Update your tracker.

Old Price: $9.99 per month

New Price: $12.99 per month

A tracker is useful only when it reflects current information.

What If You Cancel a Subscription?

You do not necessarily need to delete it.

Change its status to Canceled and record a cancellation date if that information is useful to you.

Subscription Cost Status Cancellation Date
Fitness App $10/month Canceled March 12
Music Service $10/month Active —

What If You Pause a Subscription?

You may want to record:

  • Pause date
  • Expected restart date
  • Expected charge when service resumes

Excel Beginner Tip: Monthly and Annual Calculations

For a monthly subscription:

Annual Cost = Monthly Cost × 12

An Excel formula might look like:

=B2*12

For an annual subscription:

Average Monthly Cost = Annual Cost ÷ 12

An Excel formula might look like:

=B2/12
Beginner Rule: If a cell already contains a formula beginning with an equals sign, avoid typing over it unless you know what that cell is designed to do.

Expected Cost vs. Actual Cost

A subscription list tells you what you expect to spend.

A payment log tells you what actually happened.

Price increases, temporary discounts, refunds, or billing changes can cause the two totals to differ.

Don't Confuse a Subscription With Every Recurring Bill

Not every recurring expense is necessarily a subscription.

Electricity, rent, mortgages, and similar expenses may belong in different sections of your monthly budget.

A subscription tracker is usually most useful for recurring services, memberships, apps, software, and similar ongoing purchases.

How Subscriptions Connect to Your Monthly Budget

Suppose your tracker shows:

Monthly Subscriptions = $85

Your monthly budget can then include:

Subscriptions — $85
Important idea: Different spreadsheets can do different jobs while still working together.

Keep Sensitive Information Out of Your Subscription Tracker

Your subscription tracker generally does not need:
  • Passwords
  • Full credit-card numbers
  • Debit-card PINs
  • Card security codes
  • Bank passwords
  • Social Security numbers
  • Security-question answers
Simple labels such as PayPal, Debit Card A, or Visa ending 1234 are usually enough for organization.

Common Subscription-Tracking Mistakes

Relying Only on Memory

Automatic charges are easy to forget. Use actual transaction records when building your first list.

Forgetting Annual Subscriptions

Annual subscriptions can be especially easy to overlook because the charge happens only once a year.

Ignoring Free Trials

A free trial today may become a paid subscription later, depending on the provider's terms.

Forgetting Price Increases

Update your tracker when prices change.

Deleting Canceled Subscriptions Immediately

Keeping them marked as canceled can preserve useful history.

Recording Sensitive Information

You do not need passwords or complete account credentials in the tracker.

Assuming Small Charges Don't Matter

Small subscriptions may be completely worthwhile. The point is to understand the total cost.

Canceling Something Just Because It's on the List

Tracking is about making informed decisions, not automatically eliminating everything enjoyable.

A Simple Monthly Subscription Review

Once a month, ask:

  1. Did I add any new subscriptions?
  2. Did any prices change?
  3. Are any free trials ending soon?
  4. Are any annual renewals approaching?
  5. Did I cancel or pause anything?
  6. Am I still using the services I'm paying for?
  7. Does my monthly budget still include the correct subscription total?

What Happens When You Cancel Several Subscriptions?

Suppose you cancel three services costing:

$5 + $10 + $15 = $30 per month

Over approximately one year:

$30 × 12 = $360

This does not mean everyone should cancel $30 worth of subscriptions. It simply demonstrates why knowing your recurring costs is useful.

A Subscription Tracker Is Really an Awareness Tool

The purpose of tracking subscriptions is not to make you feel guilty about paying for entertainment, software, music, games, education, or anything else you value.

A subscription may be completely worthwhile.

The problem is not knowing what you're paying for.

You may discover a service you love and use every day.

Great.

You may discover another service you have not used in eight months.

That's useful information too.

The spreadsheet does not make the decision. It helps you see the information you need to make the decision.

Start With One List

If you've never tracked subscriptions before, don't make it complicated.

Start by answering:

  • What is it?
  • How much does it cost?
  • How often am I charged?
  • When does it renew?
  • Does it automatically renew?
  • Am I still using it?

Then review the list periodically.

That's enough to begin.
